`Clock` counts elapsed time in integer AudioContext frames (context readings snapped to the 128-frame render-quantum lattice); a `seekTo(seconds)` target round-trips bit-exactly through `getTime()`, and the seconds and frame views cannot drift apart across pause/resume cycles. New: `Clock.getFrame()` / `getFrameExact()` / `seekToFrame()` / `secondsToFrame()` / `frameToSeconds()` / `sampleRate` / `rebaseCount` / `lastRebaseFrames`, `Transport.getCurrentFrame()` / `getCurrentSample()` / `rebaseCount`, the `ContextFrame` brand (distinct from `Sample`, which is the SampleTimeline's rate), the `discontinuity` transport event (`{ seconds, shiftSeconds }`), and the exported `RENDER_QUANTUM_FRAMES`. A context reading that goes backwards while playing (defensive — the spec makes `currentTime` monotonic) is absorbed by moving the anchor so the position holds; the transport then re-creates every scheduled source from the held position (clamped to the loop start; during a count-in it drops the already-armed clicks instead) and emits `discontinuity`, or warns once to the console when nobody subscribes. A stalled reading simply freezes the position. `toAudioTime()` is anchor-based in both states and never re-reads `currentTime` while running. The Transport rejects an AudioContext without a finite positive `sampleRate` (a `{ currentTime }`-only mock) separately from `options.sampleRate`, and `play()`/`seek()` reject non-finite positions before touching playback. Existing seconds-based APIs keep their signatures.
